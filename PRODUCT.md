# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individuals managing their personal tasks. They open Juice when they need to capture, organize, or tick off something — no team coordination, no complex workflows. The context is quick: capture a thought before it disappears, check what's due today, mark something done for the satisfaction of it.

## Product Purpose

A beautiful, calm task management app for personal use. Juice exists to make task management feel lighter — warm, tactile, pleasant to open. Success looks like someone choosing to open Juice before they open Notes or their default reminders app, because it feels good, not just functional.

## Brand Personality

Warm, minimal, calm. The app should feel like a well-worn leather notebook, not a spreadsheet. Approachable without being cutesy, crafted without being precious.

## Anti-references

- TickTick / Todoist / any dense, utilitarian task manager — feature overload, zero personality, looks like enterprise software
- Apple Reminders — too basic, no design opinion, feels system-level and forgettable
- Notion / Linear — too sharp and professional; Juice is for personal life, not work
- "Project management" aesthetics — Gantt charts, kanban boards, status pipelines, burndown graphs

## Design Principles

1. **Calm over feature density** — Every feature justifies its place. No bloat, no config overload, no "power user" modes. The app should get quieter as you use it.

2. **Warmth in every surface** — The UI should feel physically warm, not clinical. Tinted neutrals, soft shadows, surfaces that feel like paper or leather, not glass or metal.

3. **Moments of satisfaction** — Completing a task should feel good. Small crafted touches — a satisfying animation, a sound (optional), a visual reward — that make task management less like chores.

4. **Zero-friction capture** — The primary action on every screen is adding a task. The UI stays out of the way between capture and completion. No modals for simple actions, no extra clicks.

5. **Physical metaphor, not digital default** — Shadows have weight. Surfaces have depth. The app borrows from the physical world (notebooks, cards, paper) rather than from other apps.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard web best practices. Semantic HTML, keyboard-navigable, reasonable contrast ratios. No formal WCAG level targeted but no barriers introduced. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
